The Finding — Read This First

GEO readiness determines who controls the message. On a specific, decision-relevant question, a well-optimized site gets cited as the source of truth. A blocked or poorly-structured site doesn't disappear — the AI finds someone else's description of it instead, and cites that. You don't stop being findable. You stop being the one who gets to describe yourself.

Proof — Broad Query (Legacy Wins Here, Not GEO)

Asked cold/live on three engines: "What are some good national homebuilders to consider for a first-time home purchase in Texas?"

D.R. Horton — 0/2 on the technical audit, blocked from crawling outright — was named on all three engines and was the top pick on one. Pulte — 10/10 on the technical audit — was absent from two of three. K. Hovnanian was absent from all three — not a technical block, a brand-awareness gap. Brand scale and legacy coverage dominate this kind of question, not GEO readiness.

Proof — Specific Query (This Is Where GEO Actually Matters)

Asked cold/live on three engines, twice — D.R. Horton vs. Lennar, then K. Hovnanian vs. Lennar, both in DFW.

Gemini cited lennar.com and khov.com directly, repeatedly, favorably — but for D.R. Horton, cited third-party sources instead, never drhorton.com. The difference: D.R. Horton is technically blocked (robots.txt). K. Hovnanian's Terms restrict scraping too, but isn't technically blocked — and it shows: engines still access and cite khov.com directly.

What This Means for K. Hovnanian Specifically

Two different problems, two different fixes. K. Hovnanian is not technically blocked — both ChatGPT and Gemini cited khov.com directly and described it favorably ("more intentionally designed" vs. Lennar) on the specific query. That's a real asset already working. The gap is upstream: absent from all three engines on the broad, top-of-funnel question where D.R. Horton and Pulte both showed up. That's not a robots.txt problem — it's a brand-salience problem, and no technical fix solves it alone.

What This Audit Does and Doesn't Measure
  • Measures: technical readiness — whether AI and search engines can access, parse, and extract this site's content.
  • Does not measure: live AI-citation share — how often a brand is actually mentioned in ChatGPT/Perplexity/Gemini answers. That requires paid tools with API access at scale (Profound, Gumshoe, Semrush AI visibility).
  • ToS restrictions vs. technical blocks are different levers. Both K. Hovnanian and Lennar restrict scraping in their Terms — a legal signal — but neither is technically blocked at the robots.txt level, and both got cited directly by live engines. D.R. Horton's block is technical, not just legal, and that's what actually stopped direct citation.
  • Known limitation: a compliant robots.txt is not proof of actual access — CDN/firewall-level blocking (e.g., Cloudflare's AI Bot Management) can override it entirely before a bot's request ever reaches the server.
