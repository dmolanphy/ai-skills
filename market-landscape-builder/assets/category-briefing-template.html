Market Landscape Briefing

U.S. New Home Construction

Category briefing built via market-landscape-builder — five evidence lenses triangulated, opportunity sized and shown with its formula.
✅ Market-Validated — 5/5 lenses

New construction has quietly become cost-competitive with existing homes for the first time in years, while a flat-to-shrinking industry means builders are fighting over the same buyer pool — and none of them are messaging the one fact that could unlock the largest underserved segment.

📊Market Sizing
This industry isn't growing — builders compete for a fixed pool, not category expansion.
$169.8B industry size, -0.1% YoY (IBISWorld, Feb 2026). Starts essentially flat at 940K units, +1% (NAHB, 2026 forecast).
💭Attitudinal
Builders and buyers are both pessimistic, but about different problems.
Builder confidence below breakeven every month since April 2024, sitting at 37–39 (NAHB/Wells Fargo HMI). Only ~25% of buyers think it's a good time to buy (Fannie Mae, Feb 2025); 56% call finding the right property the hardest step (NAR 2025).
🖱️Behavioral
Research has moved online and, increasingly, into AI tools — but the close stays human.
52% found their home online, 70% searched via mobile (NAR 2025). 20% used AI chatbots for research — 28% Millennials, 32% Gen Z — mainly for affordability and neighborhood questions (Bank of America, 2026).
💰Economic
The "new costs more" assumption no longer reliably holds.
Median new-home price ($403,200) now sits below median existing-home price ($404,600) — 4th straight quarter (NAHB, Q1 2026). ~40% of builders cutting prices; many offering rate buydowns.
🔍Competitive Artifact — Tier 3, corroborated independently
Every major builder competes on customization or bundling. None lead with price parity.
See breakdown below.
Competitive Artifacts
KB Home
Built-to-Order model — structural personalization via physical Design Studio, unusual at entry-level price points.
D.R. Horton
"Home is Connected" smart-home package (Qolsys panel, video doorbell, smart thermostat) bundled as standard, not upsell.
Lennar
"Everything's Included" bundling plus an online design-quiz tool guiding selections before a community visit.
Toll Brothers
$50K+ structural design credits at the luxury tier — explicitly permits floor-plan changes volume builders refuse.
PulteGroup
Multi-brand segmentation by life stage (Pulte Homes / Centex / Del Webb) rather than one brand for everyone.
